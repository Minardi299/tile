Gạch Bông

Indochine cement tiles, drawn from a seed. Type any text: the same text always gives the same tile, and a new text gives a new pattern and palette.

About gạch bông

The tiles

Gạch bông means “flower tiles”. These cement tiles cover the floors of old houses, shops and pagodas in Vietnam and Cambodia. The technique came to the region in the French colonial era.

Each tile is made by hand. A worker pours colored cement into a brass mold with thin walls, one color per cell. A press then packs a plain cement backing onto the colored layer. The tile cures in water and never goes into a kiln.

This process gives the tiles their look: flat, matte color and hard edges where two colors meet. The full pattern only shows when many tiles lie together.

The generator

The generator copies these features of real tiles:

  • A small palette of 3 to 5 mineral colors, such as ochre, brick red, deep green, indigo and cream.
  • Square symmetry: a tile looks the same after a quarter turn or a mirror flip.
  • Quarter motifs in the corners, which join into full medallions where four tiles meet.
  • Four-tile designs, where each tile is one quarter of a large cross. About a third of the seeds make one.
  • Thin bands of a contrasting color around petals and rings.
  • A worn surface with grain, faded areas and slightly uneven edges.

Your text becomes a number, and that number makes every choice: palette, layout, petals, arms and wear. The same text always gives the same tile.
